The brief
"Based on the council report on RPA + AI foot-in-the-door ventures, come up with 5 business automation ideas — exploring RPA with AI across different markets / industries."
One wedge · five doors

These are not five businesses. They are the same machine-verified recovery wedge pointed at five industries.

The council's conclusion holds: own a machine-verified outcome, proven against a free external answer key, priced on gain-share, and made durable by operating the process so revenue recurs. What changes across industries is only the answer key (a contract, a PO, a rate card) and how hard it is to win the first data-access "yes." Every idea below is the same shape in a different vertical — so the real choice is which industry lets a no-name solo land the first deal fastest, not which idea is prettiest.

Confidence: medium-high on the structure (each idea reuses the council's four converging tests) · medium on per-industry execution (the binding constraint — the first data-access yes — is industry-specific and unsolved). Market figures are external estimates with wide ranges; treat as order-of-magnitude.

The slate — five ideas, one taxonomy

Each idea owns a backward-looking financial-recovery outcome: it re-checks transactions against a frozen, externally-supplied document and recovers what the counterparty over-billed. All are low-blast-radius (read-only, no payroll / payments / PHI), so a judgment-proof solo can carry the liability.

The shared blueprint — the four tests every idea passes

The council found four uncorrelated reasons the winning shape works. These ideas were generated to satisfy all four by construction:

Test 1 — free answer key

Verification closes itself against a document the customer already owns (a contract, PO, or rate card). It makes the outcome cheap to prove and unarguable in a gain-share dispute — the invoice either matches the frozen number or it doesn't.

Test 2 — recurrence & bounded tail

Revenue must recur and liability stay bounded. Recurrence comes from operating the audit process every cycle (not a one-time clawback); the read-only, parallel-run posture caps the downside for a <$50k solo.

Test 3 — software, not staffing

Per-transaction human touch must fall as volume rises. Because the answer key is machine-diffable, the exception queue is the shrinking residual — not the whole job. If touch-minutes stay flat, it's a BPO, and a solo loses that game.

Test 4 — winnable first "yes"

The council's #1 missed point: distribution is the unpriced gate. Contingency pricing ($0 until recovered) removes the buyer's risk, and a dead / abandoned bot is a permissionless cold-start — nobody defends a corpse.

Freight / LogisticsFAP market ~$0.97B (2025) → ~$1.9B (2030), ~14% CAGRIncumbents: Cass, Intelligent Audit, Loop, Trax
Free answer key: the shipper's negotiated carrier rate agreement. Every invoice is diffed against the contracted rate, accessorials, and fuel surcharge tables — overcharges are carrier-favoring, so the error pool is adversarial and self-renewing (it won't quietly heal).
Lazarus entryA mid-market shipper with a recently abandoned freight-audit bot, or a renewal cliff on an over-priced one. The dead bot's own rate-matching spec is half the answer key, supplied free. Pure contingency, $0 until you recover.
Graceful Exit (durable form)Once you've proven recoveries on history, take over the ongoing pre-payment audit — you operate the freight-pay gate every week, so revenue recurs rather than ending with the clawback.
Blast radiusRead-only audit of invoices; you recommend, the client pays. No write access to the TMS, no funds movement. Liability bounded.
Software vs. staffingSOFTWARE Touch-minutes fall hard — the rate table is a clean diff. This is the council's already-validated pick (28/30 in the deep-dive).
2 · Phantom PO — manufacturing procure-to-pay recovery
Manufacturing / IndustrialAP recovery-audit ~$1.19B (2025) → ~$1.79B (2034)Incumbents: PRGX, apexanalytix
Free answer key: the purchase order + goods-receipt note. A three-way match (PO ↔ receipt ↔ invoice) is the textbook frozen answer — it catches duplicate payments, price variances above contract, and invoices for goods never received.
Lazarus entryManufacturers run dense Blue Prism / SAP estates ($15–18k per digital worker / yr); P2P-matching bots that broke after an ERP update are common corpses. Resurrect the match outcome against the customer's own PO data — never copy the vendor's licensed bot code.
Graceful Exit (durable form)Move from a one-time look-back to operating the continuous match-and-flag gate on new invoices. Recovery audit is an accepted, contingency-priced category, so the buyer already understands the deal.
Blast radiusBackward-looking, read-only; no ERP write access needed. Recovers cash already out the door.
Software vs. staffingSOFTWARE Match logic templates across suppliers. Risk: PRGX / apexanalytix own the enterprise — aim at the under-served mid-market gap.
3 · Shortage Defense — retail deduction & chargeback recovery  fastest first-yes
Retail / CPG suppliersDeductions consume 3–8% of vendor sales; Amazon ~7%Incumbents: SupplyPike / Carbon6 (SPS Commerce)
Free answer key: the retailer's own deduction reason code, checked against the supplier's ASN / EDI 856, bill of lading, and proof of delivery. If the docs show the goods shipped on time and complete, the deduction is provably invalid — disputes win 40–60% of the time.
Lazarus entryMost accessible buyer on the slate: small / mid CPG brands selling into Walmart, Amazon, Target. ~90% report deductions they believe are wrong; claim windows are 30–90 days, so the pain is acute and time-boxed — ideal for a cold pitch on pure contingency.
Graceful Exit (durable form)Deductions never stop — structurally recurring. You operate the brand's deduction-dispute desk every week, so it's an ongoing operated process, not a one-shot.
Blast radiusYou file disputes with evidence the client owns; no system write access. Downside is a rejected claim, not a lawsuit.
Software vs. staffingBORDERLINE→SOFTWARE Evidence assembly can be human-heavy, but it templates per retailer (same codes, same proofs) — the curve bends as you add brands on the same retailer.
4 · Ghost Circuit — telecom / utility expense recovery
Telecom / Utility (enterprise buyers)TEM market ~$5.1–5.6B (2026), ~14% CAGRIncumbents: Tangoe, Sakon, Calero, RadiusPoint
Free answer key: the master service agreement rate card + the service inventory. Diff every line of every invoice against contracted rates and the list of circuits / lines actually in use — ~85% of telecom invoices carry errors averaging 7–12% overcharge, plus "ghost" circuits billed long after they were cancelled.
Lazarus entryHarder cold-start (enterprise procurement, longer cycle), but the error rate is so high the contingency math sells itself: $50k–200k / yr leaks at a typical enterprise. A stalled internal "telecom-audit bot" project is a credible Lazarus opening.
Graceful Exit (durable form)Strongest recurrence on the slate: TEM is inherently a monthly operated process — you validate the bill every cycle and split the ongoing savings. You become the bill-validation layer, not a one-time auditor.
Blast radiusRead-only invoice validation; you flag, the client disputes / cancels. No payments executed by you.
Software vs. staffingSOFTWARE Rate-card diff is clean; cramming disputes need some human follow-up, but the detection (where the money is) is fully machine-checkable.
5 · Contract Sentinel — healthcare supply price-compliance recovery  most durable, slowest door
Healthcare providers (supply chain, NOT clinical)Healthcare SCM ~$3.95B (2025) → ~$6.3B (2034)Incumbents: apexanalytix, PRGX
Free answer key: the GPO / manufacturer contract price file. Med-surg invoices are diffed against contracted tier pricing — price-variance leakage and lapsed rebates are recovered. The design choice that makes this viable for a solo: it operates on purchasing data, never patient data, so it sidesteps the HIPAA / PHI radioactivity the council flagged.
Lazarus entrySlowest first-yes (procurement gatekeepers, GPO complexity), but the no-PHI scope dramatically lowers the liability and compliance bar that otherwise rules healthcare out for a solo. Best entered later, with a track record from ideas 1–3 behind you.
Graceful Exit (durable form)Operate continuous purchase-price-variance monitoring against the live contract catalog — recurring, and demand is durable (relentless cost pressure on providers).
Blast radiusPurchasing / contract data only; read-only; no PHI, no payments. This is the whole point — it keeps a healthcare play inside a solo's risk budget.
Software vs. staffingSOFTWARE Contract-price diff is machine-clean. Most defensible long-term corpus (labeled price-error map), but gated behind the hardest distribution.

The recommendation — sequence, don't pick

Per the council: it's one business entered through the cheapest door. Across industries, that means enter where the first "yes" is cheapest, then climb the ACV ladder once you have cash, a track record, and a recovery corpus.

  1. Wedge (cheapest door): Shortage Defense or Deadhead. Retail deductions and freight audit are contingency-native with acute, time-boxed pain and the smallest, most reachable buyers. Land one. Instrument per-transaction touch-minutes from day one.
  2. Climb (operate to recur): turn the look-back into the gate. In whichever you entered, move from one-time recovery to operating the audit every cycle — that is the Graceful Exit where revenue recurs and you own the workflow.
  3. Expand ACV: Phantom PO and Ghost Circuit. With a track record, the same engine points at manufacturing P2P and telecom expense — bigger contracts, longer cycles, higher leak per account.
  4. Durable endgame: Contract Sentinel. Healthcare purchasing (no PHI) is the most defensible long-term corpus; enter it last, when your name opens the procurement door it can't open today.
The one thing to do first

Pick one industry from the top of the cold-start ranking and land a single contingency deal on one stable, high-volume, low-blast-radius recovery process. Re-implement the logic from the customer's own documents (rate card, PO, deduction codes, contract) — never the vendor's licensed bot code. That one deal tests all three unknowns at once: can a no-name win the access + contract, does the human-touch curve bend, and is the free answer key real and not legally radioactive.

Kill-switches (carried from the council)
  • Per-transaction human-touch-minutes stay flat across the first cohort → it's staffing, not software. Stop — a solo can't win a labor-arbitrage BPO.
  • Recovering requires copying the vendor's licensed bot IP → legal one-way door. Restructure as customer-documentation-only.
  • Can't win a first data-access "yes" within runway → the binding constraint is distribution. Switch to the next industry up the ranking where a warm intro exists.
  • The "answer key" turns out to be contractually confidential or unavailable → the cheap-verification thesis collapses for that industry; move to one where the customer owns and can share the document.
